## Full Post

> Maya joined when we had 11 customers and no onboarding process.
>
> Her title was operations lead. Her actual job was remembering every promise the company had made and quietly making sure we kept it.
>
> On Friday, she starts a new chapter.
>
> The usual words are too small. She was not the person who held the system together. She was the person who made the system teachable.
>
> When a customer escalated, she wrote down the decision rule, not just the answer. When a handoff broke, she added one sentence to the checklist and removed three. When I repeated the same mistake twice, she sent me the note she had already written for the next person who would make it.
>
> That work rarely looked urgent. It looked like a document open in the background, a calendar block called cleanup, a message that started with, "I noticed this might bite us later."
>
> It compounded. New hires reached useful independence faster. Customers stopped hearing five versions of the same policy. I stopped being the only person who knew why certain exceptions existed.
>
> The best operators do not make themselves indispensable. They make the next decision easier to inherit.
>
> If you have worked with someone like that, tell them while they are still in the room.
>
> Who made your team easier to inherit?
