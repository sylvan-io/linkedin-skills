## Full Post

> $18,742. That is what we spent to learn that our launch was aimed at the wrong buyer.
>
> The product worked. The landing page converted. The problem was that the people clicking had no authority to solve the problem we had built for.
>
> Here is the ledger.
>
> Contract researcher: $6,800
> Landing-page copy: $3,200
> Design and build: $2,400
> Paid traffic test: $3,157
> Tools and subscriptions: $1,935
> Customer interview incentives: $1,250
>
> Total: $18,742.
>
> The paid test produced 214 visits, 19 email captures, and 2 sales calls.
>
> We celebrated the capture rate for about six hours. Then both calls asked whether the tool could be approved by procurement. We had written the page for the person with the daily pain. The purchase decision belonged to their operations director, who never visited the site.
>
> The expensive part was not ad spend. It was the story built around the wrong buyer.
>
> We rewrote the offer for the operations director. Same product, different opening claim. The next test spent $840, produced 7 calls, and closed 2 pilots.
>
> The lesson is now part of our launch checklist. Before writing a headline, name the person who can say yes, the person who can say no, and the person who has to live with the result.
>
> What is the most expensive assumption your last launch tested?
