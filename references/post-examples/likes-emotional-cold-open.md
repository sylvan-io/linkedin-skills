## Full Post

> The room stayed quiet after I said the words "we are eliminating six roles."
>
> I had rehearsed the explanation for three days. Market shift. Runway. The need to make the company smaller before it could be healthy again. The language was accurate. It was also completely useless to the people hearing their names.
>
> What I remember is the chair. One person pulled it closer to the table, as if sitting straighter would change the sentence. Another asked whether last week's work mattered. A third asked how soon the health insurance ended.
>
> We spent the next two days doing the unglamorous part. We gave people time to ask the same question twice. We wrote recommendation notes before anyone asked. We told the remaining team exactly what had changed and what had not. We canceled a launch because pretending everything was normal would have been a lie.
>
> I made mistakes too. I moved through the first conversation too quickly because I wanted to stop causing pain. The people in the room needed me to stay in it.
>
> The company survived that quarter. That is not the lesson.
>
> The lesson is that a hard decision does not become compassionate because the reasoning is sound. Care shows up in the details people have to live with after the meeting ends.
